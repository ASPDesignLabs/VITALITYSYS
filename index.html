Vitals, not checklists.

A phone app and a standalone Wear OS watch app that track meals, hydration, medication, and hygiene as one live HP number — built to make missed self-care felt, the way a video game makes low health felt, instead of sitting quietly in a to-do list.

Instead of four separate reminders you can each individually ignore, VITALITY.SYS collapses meals, medication, hydration, and hygiene into a single HP value from 0 to 100, recalculated continuously. Each of the four areas below — the app calls them protocols — can bleed HP on its own the longer it's neglected: a short grace period, then a base penalty, then a steady drip for every extra minute you're behind.

Two effects sit on top of that base math:

  • Bio-Drift. If more than one protocol is failing at the same time, the damage doesn't just add up — it compounds. Letting hydration and a medication dose slide together costs more than letting either slide alone, which mirrors how skipped self-care actually tends to cascade.
  • Overcharge. Holding 100 HP continuously for about an hour fills a second "shield" meter. It's a reward for a solid stretch, and it resets instantly the moment HP drops below 100 — no coasting on a good streak.

The number is shared live between your phone and your watch over Bluetooth (no server involved — see Privacy & offline), so the watch isn't a passive mirror of the phone. It's built to be the thing you actually glance at and act on, with the phone as the fuller dashboard and configuration surface.

Deck 02

PROTOCOLS

TAP A PROTOCOL TO EXPAND

Every protocol is user-configurable — none of this is fixed slots. Set as many meals, medications, dose times, and hygiene tasks as you actually have. The stats below are a demo, not live data.

Schedule 1–5 meal times. Logging one opens a macro-entry screen — calories, protein, fat, fiber, sugar — and writes the meal to Android Health Connect as a nutrition record.

Any number of medications, each with any number of daily doses, each tracked separately. This protocol's damage curve is the steepest of the four by design — missed doses matter most.

Each log adds a fixed 250 mL toward a target you set, paced across your own defined "active hours" window, and writes a hydration record to Health Connect.

Any number of your own named tasks — morning routine, evening routine, or whatever you actually need reminding of — each with its own scheduled time.

Every protocol's action is one tap, on phone or watch. Chemistry and Maintenance never show you a scrollable list of pending items — just the single soonest-due one, so the watch stays a quick glance instead of another list to page through.

When a protocol comes due and you open it, the phone asks one question first: what were you doing? A short free-text note, before you're allowed to mark the action done.

Once you confirm, it shows a "system optimized" screen that hands the exact thing you typed back to you before returning to the dashboard. It's built around getting pulled out of a task by a bodily need, not just clearing a reminder — the app tries to make sure you land back where you actually were.

A recreation of the watch's own pain-level selector — tapping the top or bottom edge raises or lowers the number, 0 to 10. Demo value shown, not a live reading.

A separate flow from the four protocols: log a symptom on a 0–10 scale (from resolved up through critical) with a short note on location, type, and triggers — from the watch or the phone.

Logging anything above a 3 automatically schedules a 60-minute follow-up check-in, unless it's inside your configured sleep window — which can itself be overridden when round-the-clock monitoring is actually wanted.

Deck 05

REPORTS & AUDITS

COMPLIANCE & EXPORT

Every alert the system fires is tracked end to end — issued, interacted with, fulfilled — and graded success / warning / failure, rolled up into a per-protocol A–F compliance score.

From the phone you can generate a multi-page PDF report, in either the app's own dark "system" theme or a plain light "clinical" theme meant for sharing with a doctor or care team. It covers the raw event log, 24-hour/7-day/30-day summaries, per-protocol response latency, which hour of the day tends to see the most abandoned alerts, and a response-time histogram — handed off through the normal Android share sheet.

VITALITY.SYS ships as a standalone Wear OS app — it keeps running its own schedule and alerts even when the phone is out of Bluetooth range, then re-syncs once it's back.

Navigation is built for the bezel: rotate to move between five decks (one per protocol, plus pain logging), or tap the top and bottom edges of the screen. Each deck shows exactly one piece of live status and one action button — nothing to scroll through.

Deck 07 // Privacy & Offline

ADMIN // DATA_GOVERNANCE_PROTOCOL

"VITALITY.SYS operates on a strict self-sovereign data matrix... telemetry regarding hydration and macromolecular ingestion is syndicated strictly locally to your device's core repository. We do not broadcast your physiological status to megacorps." — the app's own Health Connect permissions screen

Neither app requests the INTERNET permission, and there is no networking code anywhere in either module. The only channel between phone and watch is Bluetooth, and Health Connect writes stay on-device. Everything else — logs, audits, daily stats, your schedule — lives in a local database and SharedPreferences. No accounts, no cloud sync, no analytics or crash-reporting SDKs.

The one admin/debug panel in the codebase (data resets, synthetic test alerts) doesn't exist at all in a release build — it's compiled out unless the APK is built as a debug variant, and even then it stays off until explicitly switched on for testing.

Deck 08

SYS_INFO // SOURCE_ARCHIVE

Part of a larger system: Overseer

VITALITY.SYS doesn't run in isolation on the watch. It broadcasts its live HP and status locally to a sibling project, Overseer, and can be paused by it — the two are designed as modules in one broader personal system rather than as one all-in-one app.

Overseer has its own project page: aspdesignlabs.github.io/OVERSEER.

Get it

There's no Play Store listing. Build it yourself from source with Gradle, or watch the repo's Releases page for prebuilt APKs.
