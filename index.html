Vitals, not checklists.

A phone app and a standalone Wear OS watch app that track meals, hydration, medication, and hygiene as one live HP number — built to make missed self-care felt, the way a video game makes low health felt, instead of sitting quietly in a to-do list.

Instead of four separate reminders you can each individually ignore, VITALITY.SYS collapses meals, medication, hydration, and hygiene into a single HP value from 0 to 100, recalculated continuously. Each of the four areas below — the app calls them protocols — can bleed HP on its own the longer it's neglected: a short grace period, then a base penalty, then a steady drip for every extra minute you're behind.

Two effects sit on top of that base math:

  • Bio-Drift. If more than one protocol is failing at the same time, the damage doesn't just add up — it compounds. Letting hydration and a medication dose slide together costs more than letting either slide alone, which mirrors how skipped self-care actually tends to cascade.
  • Overcharge. Holding 100 HP continuously for about an hour fills a second "shield" meter. It's a reward for a solid stretch, and it resets instantly the moment HP drops below 100 — no coasting on a good streak.

The number is shared live between your phone and your watch over Bluetooth (no server involved — see Privacy & Offline, further down), so the watch isn't a passive mirror of the phone. It's built to be the thing you actually glance at and act on, with the phone as the fuller dashboard and configuration surface.

Deck 02

PROTOCOLS

TAP A PROTOCOL TO EXPAND

Every protocol is user-configurable — none of this is fixed slots. Set as many meals, medications, dose times, and hygiene tasks as you actually have. The stats below are a demo, not live data.

Schedule 1–5 meal times. Logging one opens a macro-entry screen — calories, protein, fat, fiber, sugar — and writes the meal to Android Health Connect as a nutrition record.

Any number of medications, each with any number of daily doses, each tracked separately. This protocol's damage curve is the steepest of the four by design — missed doses matter most.

Each log adds a fixed 250 mL toward a target you set, paced across your own defined "active hours" window, and writes a hydration record to Health Connect.

Any number of your own named tasks — morning routine, evening routine, or whatever you actually need reminding of — each with its own scheduled time.

Every protocol's action is one tap, on phone or watch. Chemistry and Maintenance never show you a scrollable list of pending items — just the single soonest-due one, so the watch stays a quick glance instead of another list to page through.
